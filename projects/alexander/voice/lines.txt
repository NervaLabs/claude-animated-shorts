1|It's 356 BC, in the kingdom of Macedonia, and a prince named Alexander is born.
2|His father, King Philip the Second, is turning Macedonia into a military powerhouse.
2|As a boy, Alexander tames a wild horse nobody else can ride, just by turning it toward the sun, because it was scared of its own shadow.
2|So Philip hires the smartest man in Greece to teach him. Aristotle.
2|Philosophy, mathematics, medicine, and how to lead an army.
3|Then, in 336 BC, Philip is assassinated by one of his own bodyguards, at a wedding.
3|Suddenly, Alexander is king. He's twenty years old.
4|And he has a plan: invade the Persian Empire, the biggest superpower on Earth.
4|At Issus, he charges straight at the Persian king, Darius, who turns his chariot around and runs.
4|Two years later, at Gaugamela, Darius brings a much bigger army.
4|Alexander punches a hole right through the middle, and Darius runs away again.
5|Egypt welcomes him as a pharaoh. Persia falls.
5|He keeps marching, through Central Asia and all the way into India, until his exhausted army finally refuses to go any further.
6|Back in Egypt, in 331 BC, he had marked out a new city with barley flour, because the builders ran out of chalk.
6|Birds ate the whole plan. His priests called it a great sign, and they were right. Alexandria became one of the greatest cities on Earth.
7|Then, in 323 BC, in Babylon, Alexander catches a fever.
7|Days later, he's dead, at just thirty-two.
8|Asked who should rule his empire, he reportedly answered: the strongest.
8|So his generals fought over it, and it shattered into rival kingdoms.
8|But Greek language, art, and ideas now stretched from Egypt to India.
8|Nearly three hundred years later, Egypt's last queen still spoke Greek. Her name? Cleopatra.
8|All because one boy from Macedonia never lost a battle.
