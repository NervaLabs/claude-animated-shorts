# Fact notes: batch 2 (100 scripts)

The videos narrate the scripts as written. This list flags claims that historians dispute, call myths, or that are simplified. If a short gets traction, these are the ones commenters may challenge. Fix the script text before re-rendering if you want them airtight.

## Likely myths or unsupported

| # | Claim | Note |
|---|---|---|
| 017 | Terracotta warriors used a chromium anti-rust coating | Recent research (2019) found the chromium came from the lacquer on the weapons' handles, not a deliberate coating. Blade preservation is put down to bronze composition and the soil. |
| 040 | Henry VIII / Elizabeth I beard tax | No primary record of a Tudor beard tax has been found. It's widely repeated but treated as a legend. Peter the Great's 1698 Russian beard tax *is* real. |
| 062 | John Quincy Adams kept Lafayette's alligator in the East Room | There's no contemporary source. Historians treat it as legend, so the video adds "(SO THE STORY GOES)" on screen. |
| 014 | Romans had "no word for volcano" | Latin had *mons ignifer* / Vulcan references, and Pliny the Younger described the eruption in detail. "No concept of an active volcano" is closer. |
| 069 | Trojan Horse as a siege engine | This is a scholarly *hypothesis* (e.g. Pausanias' suggestion, horse-hide-covered rams), not established fact. The script hedges with "might", which is fine. |

## Disputed or simplified numbers

| # | Claim | Note |
|---|---|---|
| 081 | Acoustic Kitty cost $20 million | That figure comes from Victor Marchetti's recollection. Declassified documents don't confirm a cost, and estimates vary widely. |
| 044 | Great Fire "ended" the plague | It's debated: plague was already declining across Europe, and the burned area was mostly the City, not the worst-hit outer slums. "Helped end" is safer. |
| 029 | "French town" (title / script) | Strasbourg was in the Holy Roman Empire in 1518. Heart-attack deaths are reported by later chroniclers, not certain. |
| 033 | Plate armor 45–55 lb; knights doing cartwheels | The weight is right for field armour. Cartwheels come from the account of Jean II Le Maingre (Boucicaut) and are anecdotal. |
| 053 | "Dr. Miles' Compound Extract of Tomato" | This was Archibald Miles' product, launched alongside Bennett's pills, and the "craze" ran in the late 1830s. The story is broadly fine. |
| 054 | Crush, Texas: 40,000 spectators; 2 deaths | Accounts say 2–3 deaths and 30,000–40,000 spectators. |
| 055 | Napoleon "5'2" French inches" | His autopsy height was 5 pieds 2 pouces ≈ 168–169 cm (about 5'6½"). The Imperial Guard height minimum was ~5'10" for most units, not over 6 ft. |
| 063 | Tudor's first ice shipment went to Calcutta in 1806 | His first shipment (1806) went to Martinique. Calcutta came in **1833**. The script compresses the two. |
| 065 | Scheele's Green "deaths across Britain" from arsine gas | The arsine mechanism (Gosio gas) is real but its contribution is debated. Napoleon's death is often linked, but that's unproven. |
| 067 | 10,000+ Chinese laborers; basket-lowering | The workforce peaked at ~12,000 Chinese workers, which fits. The "reed baskets" account is popular but has thin primary sourcing. |
| 072 | May Pierstorff mailed 73 miles | Correct (Grangeville → Lewiston, Idaho, 1914). She rode in the mail car with a relative who worked for the postal service. |
| 076 | Emus "outran bullets even after being hit" | That's paraphrased from Major Meredith's remarks. The actual cull was ~1,000 birds of ~20,000. |
| 079 | Wojtek "500 lb"; corporal | His rank was private, later corporal (accounts vary). His weight is often given as ~485 lb (220 kg). |
| 086 | Olympic "survived the 1912 disaster because she was in the Atlantic" | Olympic was at sea and answered Titanic's distress call. In 1918 she rammed and sank U-103, which is correct. |
| 087 | Fitzpatrick "stole" the plane | It's usually described as "borrowed without permission". The 1956 and 1958 landings are documented. |
| 088 | Nintendo founded the "exact same year" the Eiffel Tower was completed | Both are 1889 (the tower in March, Nintendo in September). Calling it the "same year" is accurate. |
| 091 | Annie Londonderry "around the globe … $10,000 bet" | The bet itself may have been a publicity invention. She travelled much of the route by ship. |
| 094 | Pepsi briefly owned "one of the largest navies" | Exaggerated. The 1989 barter did include 17 old submarines plus a cruiser, frigate and destroyer, sold for scrap, but "6th-largest navy" is a joke attributed to a Pepsi exec. |
| 096 | David Blair's key sold for over $130,000 | Correct: about £90,000 in 2007. |
| 099 | Hollywood was "next to the Mexican border" | LA is ~130 miles from the border. Escaping Edison's Motion Picture Patents Company is a real factor. |

## Sensitive topics: how they were handled

- **059 (Yamaguchi):** desaturated grade, no blast or injury imagery, only a white flash and muted drone/rumble SFX.
- **070 (Lincoln):** the assassination is referenced only by a calendar date. No violence is shown.
- **054, 068, 076, 089:** deaths, surgery, shooting and nuclear threat are conveyed through captions, props (stopwatch, radar screen) or smoke, never shown on bodies.
- **039 (Zheng Yi Sao):** the "beheading" line is shown as a written code scroll only.
- **Brands (088 Nintendo, 094 Pepsi, 083 IBM):** names are spoken in the narration, but the visuals use generic stand-ins (playing cards, a soda bottle, a computer) with no logos or characters.
